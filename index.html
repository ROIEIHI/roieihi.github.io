01 · ELECTRO-OPTICS

Silicon-Photonic

Sample-Efficient Optimization of a Forward-Bias PIN Electro-Optic Phase Shifter with RC-Equalizer for Datacom Applications

Objective

Co-optimize a forward-bias PIN modulator's competing metrics - modulation efficiency and optical loss - which the plasma-dispersion effect couples into a nonlinear trade-off that one-factor sweeps cannot solve.

Result

A Gaussian-process surrogate driving Lumerical multi-physics located a valid design in 150 simulations, raising the valid-design rate from 22% to 80% and reaching VπL = 0.649 V·mm at 15.2 dB/cm, holding within ±0.26% efficiency and ±4.5% loss across the full O-band.

Bandwidth

The device's extracted small-signal parameters (CF = 0.347 pF, RF = 10.55 kΩ) set an intrinsic 6.25 GHz bandwidth; a passive RC equalizer sized by zero-pole cancellation pushed that to 24.9 GHz for 25 Gbps operation, raising the eye's Q-factor from 5.45 to 12.66.

A Lightweight RGB Webcam-Based Gaze Tracking System for Real-World Reading Analysis

Objective

Turn a standard RGB webcam into a reading-behavior analyzer with no infrared hardware, GPU, or head stabilization - overcoming webcam jitter and the weak vertical-gaze signal.

Result

A CPU-only pipeline - MediaPipe landmarks, engineered cross-term features, a multi-output XGBoost regressor, and a velocity-adaptive Kalman filter - reached 3.6° mean angular error across 187 participants, with reading metrics in the physiological range.

Validation

A 30-point calibration with an 11-frame burst per target - tuned to minimize the error/overfitting trade-off - trains the per-session regressor; the resulting fixation durations (mean 357 ms) and ~75 WPM reading speed land inside established physiological ranges.

Exposing Deepfakes Through Spectral Anomalies in Colour Channels

Objective

Test whether spectral colour-channel anomalies left by generative upsampling can separate real from fake faces - by reproducing a published method's 2D-DFT / colour-correlation pipeline and stress-testing its claims.

Result

The reproduced spectral-correlation classifiers topped out at 66.6% accuracy on compressed (c23) video, while a spatial Xception CNN control hit 80.8% accuracy / 0.93 AUC on the same data - evidence the forensic signal survives in the spatial pixels, and that the paper's stronger reported results likely reflect compression-fragile artifacts and probable frame-level data leakage rather than a robust spectral fingerprint.

Denoising Transient Power Quality Disturbances Using an Improved Adaptive Wavelet Threshold Method

Objective

Reproduce and critically evaluate a published energy-optimized, scale-adaptive wavelet thresholding method (Goh et al.) for preserving the sharp transient edges of a voltage sag while removing AWGN - a case where a single global threshold either over-smooths the event or leaves noise behind.

Result

A MATLAB pipeline reproducing the method's Peak-to-Sum-Ratio thresholds, critical-scale selection, and |D1| modulus-maxima edge detection benchmarked it against hard and soft thresholding across a 0–35 dB input-SNR sweep. The adaptive method was consistently competitive to best, but its margin over the simple baselines was narrow - a practical limitation the original paper understates.

Low-Illumination Image Enhancement: Retinex vs. Nonlinear Mapping

Objective

Reproduce and benchmark two published low-light / nonuniform-illumination enhancement algorithms - a dual-branch (log + tanh) Retinex method and a JND/Naka-Rushton-based nonlinear tone-mapping method (SNRF) - on real non-uniformly lit photos.

Result

Both algorithms were reimplemented in MATLAB and scored with Entropy, RMS contrast, PSNR, and Lightness-Order-Error (LOE) against the originals. Across test scenes, the Retinex method consistently gained more entropy (more recovered detail) but at a larger tonal deviation (lower PSNR, higher LOE), while the JND-SNRF method made a more conservative, order-preserving correction - a clear detail-vs-fidelity trade-off between the two published approaches.

915 MHz PIFA Integrated in a Boot

Objective

Design a compact PCB antenna that resonates at 915 MHz while worn on the body, where the foot is a high-loss dielectric that absorbs RF and drags the resonant frequency downward.

Result

A quarter-wave PIFA placed in the rigid heel counter - chosen over the sole and top-of-foot for mechanical robustness and radiation direction - matched near 915 MHz and simulated in HFSS with custom foot, leather, rubber, and concrete dielectrics, giving an off-body directional pattern.

Circular Waveguide Mode Analysis

Objective

Derive the TE and TM mode fields of a circular waveguide from Maxwell's equations in cylindrical coordinates, and verify the resulting cutoff-frequency and impedance predictions against full-wave simulation.

Result

A MATLAB pipeline solves for the Bessel-function roots that set each mode's cutoff wavenumber and frequency, confirming TE11 as the dominant (lowest-cutoff) mode ahead of TM01. A matching CST model reproduced the derived mode structure and port impedance, cross-checking the theoretical derivation against simulation.
